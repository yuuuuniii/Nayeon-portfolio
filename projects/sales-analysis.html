Sales Analysis: Golin Golf Wear

A Korean golf wear brand targeting customers in their 20s–30s was losing momentum in a crowding market. I ran a full EDA across three years of sales and customer feedback to find where the next product lineup should focus.

The question

Golin's growth was slowing while competition intensified. Leadership needed to know: which products, channels, and promotions should the next season's lineup bet on? The answer had to come from what customers were actually doing — not what the merchandising team assumed.

Approach

  1. Collect — consolidated three years of transactions from 20+ offline stores and 15+ online channels with 1,000+ reviews and survey responses.
  2. Clean — removed missing values, standardized formats across channel systems that recorded the same products differently.
  3. Analyze — cut the data three ways: by customer, by channel, and by promotion.
  4. Generate insights — focused on product mix and promotion strategy, the two levers merchandising could actually pull.
  5. Recommend — translated findings into a concrete lineup and bundling plan.

Key findings

  • Churn was the real problem: most new customers made a single purchase and never returned — retention, not acquisition, was the growth lever.
  • Price sensitivity varied by channel: budget items concentrated among lower-income customers in specific channels, arguing against one-size pricing.
  • Basket patterns differed by category: sweaters and vests were bought in multi-item baskets 80%+ of the time; outerwear was a single-purchase category (~40%).
  • Channel seasonality diverged: department stores spiked seasonally while online and agent channels stayed consistent — different inventory strategies needed.

Outcome

The bundling recommendation — pairing high-attachment categories with accessories — was implemented in the 21SS launch and delivered 15% sales growth. The analysis worked because it ended in a decision someone could execute, not a report.
