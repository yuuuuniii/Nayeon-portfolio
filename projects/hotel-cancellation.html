Hotel Booking Cancellation Analysis

Cancellations are lost revenue a hotel can see coming — if the model is right. I compared five classification approaches to predict which bookings would cancel, and examined where each model type breaks down.

Revenueframing for Marketing & Sales

The question

In the post-pandemic hospitality market, cancellation behavior changed — lead times, room rates, and amenities like parking availability all shifted in importance. The Marketing & Sales framing: which bookings should we treat as at-risk, so pricing and overbooking strategy can respond before the revenue is gone?

Approach

  • Explored and cleaned the booking dataset, engineering features around lead time, rate, and amenity signals that prior research links to cancellation behavior.
  • Trained and compared five classifiers: K-Nearest Neighbors, Decision Tree, Bagged Tree, Boosting, and Random Forest.
  • Evaluated the trade-offs — not just which model scored highest, but which errors each made and how interpretable its decisions were for a business audience.

What I learned about model choice

Ensemble methods (Random Forest, Boosting) delivered the strongest predictive performance, but the single Decision Tree earned its place in the final presentation: stakeholders could read its splits directly and see why a booking was flagged. The lesson that carried into my professional work: the best model is the one the decision-maker will actually act on.

Why it matters for my work

Healthcare risk models face the same tension — a care team won't trust a black box that flags their patients. Comparing model families on interpretability, not just accuracy, is exactly the judgment clinical analytics requires.
